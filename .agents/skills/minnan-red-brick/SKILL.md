# Minnan Red Brick Architecture

Use this skill when the user wants to design or revise a presentation inspired by Minnan red-brick architecture. Let content length, information density, logic, and slide purpose determine each composition. Integrate brick, stone, masonry order, and spatial atmosphere into the page's visual language instead of displaying components as objects. ## Design language

- **Color:** Build the palette around brick red, swallowtail gray, and warm ivory. Brick red establishes regional identity, gray adds restraint and structure, and ivory protects reading space. Adjust their balance to the content; avoid a single red field or letting ivory overwhelm the brick character.
- **Clay texture:** Suggest fine grains, natural firing variation, tiny pores, narrow mortar traces, and slight edge irregularity. Integrate texture into background layers, page edges, dividers, and information fields. Keep body text and annotations on stable, high-contrast areas. Do not repeatedly place loose bricks or turn brick samples into the slide subject.
- **Masonry rhythm:** Translate staggered joints, interweaving, overlap, density, and variation within repetition into hierarchy, section boundaries, chart groups, and pacing. Let content determine the combination. Do not tile the page with a repeated brick pattern or require the same motif on every slide.
- **Swallowtail cadence:** Use a gently rising curve, restrained turns, and shifts in height to guide attention or shape an edge or background layer. Suggest the rhythm rather than drawing a roof; do not make a swallowtail outline a fixed header or border.
- **Brick-stone transition:** Express brick and swallowtail gray/ivory through material transitions, offset boundaries, local depth, and contact shadows. Unless the content specifically discusses the technique, do not substitute a standalone wall photograph or material callout diagram for page design.
- **Space and light:** Use foreground/background layers, partial occlusion, perspective, soft side light, contact shadows, and whitespace to convey architectural depth and courtyard atmosphere. Spatial forms must support titles, reading areas, charts, or components. Avoid complete buildings, fortresses, panoramic walls, and isolated 3D ornament.
- **Content adaptation:** Historical development may use chronology or narrative sections; craft relationships may use comparison or process; space and climate may use an open relationship diagram; regional memory may use a concise closing. These are options, not templates. Design charts, tables, and information components around the actual relationships in the content.
- **Character and readability:** Preserve warmth, simplicity, craft, composure, and contemporary architectural editorial quality. Keep body text, small text, and chart annotations on areas with stable value and low texture. Add a light reading field or reduce local texture when needed; material effects must not compromise readability.
- **Historical scope:** Minnan regions, periods, house types, and construction practices vary. Do not generalize a documented chronology or specific spatial practice into a universal feature of every Minnan building. Modern color, texture, and composition are design interpretations, not historical reconstructions.
