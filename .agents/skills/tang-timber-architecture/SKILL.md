# Tang Timber Architecture

Use this skill when a user wants slides designed or revised in the aesthetic of Tang timber architecture. Let each page respond to the content's length, information density, relationships, and narrative purpose. Before designing, read [`assets/README.md`](assets/README.md). For historical context or architectural claims, consult [`references/sources.md`](references/sources.md) as needed.

## Visual language

- **Color relationships:** Use deep wood tones as a grounded base, cinnabar sparingly for emphasis and cadence, and earth yellow with warm ivory for warmth and readable space. Adjust the balance to the content. Avoid a full-page sepia cast, red borders, or empty flat-color backgrounds.
- **Material:** Suggest depth with naturally varied fine wood grain, shifts in timber value, and raking warm light. Integrate texture into content areas, backgrounds, and selected components; vary its strength with information density. It must not remain an isolated backdrop or overwhelm text.
- **Structural translation:** Turn the horizontal and vertical order of beams and columns into information hierarchy and alignment rhythm. Translate interlocking, bearing, and layering into relationships among components, overlap, and visual emphasis. Express the reach of eaves through horizontal movement and spatial tension. These are organizing principles, not diagrams to draw on every page.
- **Atmosphere:** Aim for a strong, open, plainspoken, stable, and spatially expansive contemporary expression. Avoid dated faux-antique styling, stacked distressed paper, over-geometric structures, literal joinery showcases, and empty slides with only a wood texture or solid color.
- **Content adaptation:** Chronology may use a timeline or period narrative; structural relationships may use hierarchy, a path, or a concise diagram; multiple heritage categories may use staggered editorial groups; dense content may use clear divisions and concise explanations. Choose components from the actual content; these are options, not templates.
- **Flexible composition:** Vary each page's focal point, horizontal or vertical direction, texture flow, grouping, and negative space. The five examples are visual evidence only; do not copy their full-page composition, background placement, wording, or annotations.
- **Legibility:** Put body text, small labels, and chart notes in stable, lower-texture areas. Keep them clear of high-contrast grain, strong light, and color boundaries. Add a stable reading field, reduce local texture, or strengthen contrast when needed.
- **Historical boundaries:** Tang timber architecture spans regions, types, and building traditions. Nanchan Temple Main Hall and Foguang Temple East Hall are specific cases, not proxies for all Tang architecture. Do not present modern design translation as historical color measurement, architectural reconstruction, or a universal typology. See `references/sources.md` and `assets/README.md` for factual boundaries and image limitations.

## Multilingual output

This section takes precedence over any other statement in this skill about language, typefaces, or reading direction.

- **Instruction language is not output language:** This file is written in English; it does not set the language of the presentation.
- **Choosing the output language automatically:** Follow the user's explicit choice. Otherwise infer it from the language and intent of the current input (slide content, request wording, context). No language is fixed, and neither the repository nor the instruction language is a default. Any language and mixed-language content are in scope.
- **Preserve the user's wording:** Never translate, rewrite, convert between Simplified and Traditional Chinese, or transliterate supplied content on your own initiative, including names, quotations, code, numbers, and units. Translate only when explicitly asked. If the requested output language differs from the supplied text and translation was not mentioned, no fixed follow-up question is needed: decide the language of newly written text from the user's input and keep the supplied text as it is.
- **Fit layout to the text:** Adjust type hierarchy, line breaks, columns, and slide count to actual text length. Do not force content in by shrinking it past legibility or clipping it.
- **Font coverage:** Use an available font that covers the target script, with no missing or invented glyphs. Where this skill names Song-style or sans-serif character, pick a serif or sans-serif of similar character for other scripts.
- **Reading direction:** For right-to-left scripts such as Arabic and Hebrew, mirror alignment, reading order, timelines, and process flow, keeping numbers, Latin words, and punctuation correct in mixed text. Use vertical setting only when the script supports it and the content calls for it.
- **Scope:** This section governs presentation content and layout. Layout advice, including calls for short captions, does not authorize cutting or condensing the user's wording. If supplied text conflicts with checkable history or this skill's historical boundaries, point it out and change it only after the user confirms.
- **Change the language, keep the style:** Preserve this skill's color, material, texture, gloss, depth, character, and atmosphere. Chinese text in example images is sample text: do not copy it or treat it as the default language.
- **Check before delivery:** Look for missing glyphs, misspellings, broken lines, punctuation, and direction errors. If image generation cannot render a script reliably, use editable text layers; if that is not possible, tell the user.
