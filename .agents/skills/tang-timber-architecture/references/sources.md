# Tang Timber Architecture: Historical Sources and Design Boundaries

## Traceable sources

- [UNESCO: Mount Wutai nomination document](https://whc.unesco.org/document/152245) — dates specific buildings including Nanchan Temple Main Hall (782) and Foguang Temple East Hall (857).
- [Beijing Daily: Foguang Temple East Hall architecture and survey history](https://xinwen.bjd.com.cn/content/s68c56460e4b0221b9becd3e0.html) — overview of inscriptions, the scripture pillar, the 1937 survey, and surviving art at Foguang Temple East Hall. Claims should remain specific to this building.
- [Study of lateral rocking resistance in hall-type timber frames](https://pubs.cstam.org.cn/article/doi/10.6052/j.issn.1000-4750.2018.09.0494) — research on a particular hall-type timber frame and its structural connections. Do not generalize an individual study into a universal model of all Tang timber architecture.

## Evidence and limits

- **Verified facts:** The sources above date Nanchan Temple Main Hall to 782 and Foguang Temple East Hall to 857. Inscriptions, the scripture pillar, and modern survey history are evidence relating to the specific Foguang Temple building.
- **Modern design translation:** Deep wood, cinnabar, earth yellow/warm ivory, grain movement, horizontal span, layered support, and raking light are contemporary presentation choices. They are not historical color measurements or reconstructions of original Tang materials and appearance.
- **Scope:** Tang timber architecture includes different regions, building types, construction traditions, and later repairs. Identify the building and source when using particular historical or structural claims; do not treat Nanchan or Foguang as universal examples.
- **Image text revision (2026-11-26):** Unverifiable source notes and a report title were removed from the example images, and statements about dating, murals, and inscriptions were rewritten to match the sources. Liang Sicheng’s study of Foguang Temple appears in volume 7 of the Bulletin of the Society for Research in Chinese Architecture; check the original before citing it.

## Design use

Organize slides through material layers, spatial order, horizontal and vertical cadence, and relationships of support. Unless the supplied content discusses a specific building or component, do not use a hall, roof, bracket set, or close-up of joinery as decoration. Structural ideas should shape information hierarchy and component relationships instead of recurring as literal architectural diagrams.
