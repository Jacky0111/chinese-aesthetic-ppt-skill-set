# Dunhuang Caves

Use this skill when the user wants to design or revise a presentation inspired by Dunhuang cave art. Let content length, information density, logic, and narrative purpose determine each composition. Maintain coherence through mineral color layers, material texture, spatial depth, and flowing rhythm. Read [`assets/README.md`](assets/README.md) before designing; consult [`references/sources.md`](references/sources.md) as needed for dates, pigments, cave structure, or Silk Road exchange.

## Design language

- **Color:** Build a muted palette from sand yellow, ochre red, and stone green. Create depth through tonal variation, adjacent-color transitions, and localized pigment deposits. Adjust proportions to the content; do not present one cave or period's historical pigments as a universal palette. This palette is a modern design interpretation, not historical color reconstruction.
- **Mural and rock texture:** Suggest fine mineral granulation, matte pigment layers, natural color variation, subtle erosion, and rock grain. Keep the surface refined and restrained. Avoid distressed filters, dirty noise, torn-paper effects, or cheap cracks; do not reduce the style to a flat sand-yellow background.
- **Space and light:** Use foreground/background layering, partial occlusion, gradual depth, soft raking light, and whitespace to evoke a quiet, deep space. Do not draw cave entrances, repeated openings, cliffside cave façades, earthen forts, battlements, watchtowers, city walls, or other fortress-like architecture. Do not show a complete cave building.
- **Compositional rhythm:** Abstract the inward order, hierarchy, and geometric cadence of caisson ceilings into information grouping or local focal structure. Do not reproduce a specific caisson pattern or force circles and symmetry onto every slide. Translate the expansiveness and motion of apsara art into gentle lines, color flow, or page rhythm—never into apsara figures or literal ribbons.
- **Content adaptation:** Use timelines, narrative progression, or comparisons for historical development; material layering, process diagrams, or comparisons for pigment and conservation topics; and relationship paths, networks, or spatial storytelling for Silk Road exchange. These are options, not templates. Choose charts, tables, cards, and components from the actual content.
- **Imagery boundary:** Do not use Buddha figures, apsaras, lotus flowers, Buddhist symbols, complete murals, cave exteriors, or fortress-like forms as decoration. If the input must discuss one of these subjects, prefer text or abstract structure; consider literal depiction only if the user explicitly requests it, with source and scope checked.
- **Typography and readability:** A restrained Song-style display face may suit headings; use a clear modern typeface for body text and labels. Choose from fonts available in the environment; do not depend on personal font installations. Keep body text, footnotes, and chart labels on stable, low-texture fields away from dense grain, highlights, and color boundaries. Add a reading field, reduce local texture, or increase whitespace as needed.
- **Historical scope:** Specific caves, periods, pigments, and conservation states vary. Do not generalize features from one cave to all Dunhuang caves. Colors, textures, and spatial treatments in generated examples are modern interpretations, not artifact images, mural reconstructions, or restorations of historical appearance. See [`references/sources.md`](references/sources.md).

## Multilingual output

This section takes precedence over any other statement in this skill about language, typefaces, or reading direction.

- **Instruction language is not output language:** This file is written in English; it does not set the language of the presentation.
- **Choosing the output language automatically:** Follow the user's explicit choice. Otherwise infer it from the language and intent of the current input (slide content, request wording, context). No language is fixed, and neither the repository nor the instruction language is a default. Any language and mixed-language content are in scope.
- **Preserve the user's wording:** Never translate, rewrite, convert between Simplified and Traditional Chinese, or transliterate supplied content on your own initiative, including names, quotations, code, numbers, and units. Translate only when explicitly asked. Supplied text must appear character for character, including punctuation, capitalization, numbers, and units: add no terminal punctuation and swap no marks; line breaks may move but must not add or remove characters. If the requested output language differs from the supplied text and translation was not mentioned, no fixed follow-up question is needed: decide the language of newly written text from the user's input and keep the supplied text as it is.
- **Fit layout to the text:** Adjust type hierarchy, line breaks, columns, and slide count to actual text length. Do not force content in by shrinking it past legibility or clipping it.
- **Font coverage:** Use an available font that covers the target script, with no missing or invented glyphs. Where this skill names Song-style or sans-serif character, pick a serif or sans-serif of similar character for other scripts.
- **Reading direction:** For right-to-left scripts such as Arabic and Hebrew, mirror alignment, reading order, timelines, and process flow, keeping numbers, Latin words, and punctuation correct in mixed text. Use vertical setting only when the script supports it and the content calls for it.
- **Scope:** This section governs presentation content and layout. Layout advice, including calls for short captions, does not authorize cutting or condensing the user's wording. If supplied text conflicts with checkable history or this skill's historical boundaries, point it out and change it only after the user confirms.
- **Change the language, keep the style:** Preserve this skill's color, material, texture, gloss, depth, character, and atmosphere. Chinese text in example images is sample text: do not copy it or treat it as the default language.
- **Check before delivery:** When calling image generation, quote the slide text exactly in the prompt and state that no punctuation may be added, removed, or replaced. Before delivery, compare the text actually rendered with the user's text character by character, including the end of every line; fix or disclose any difference, and report what the image really shows. Also look for missing glyphs, misspellings, broken lines, and direction errors. If image generation cannot render a script reliably, use editable text layers; if that is not possible, tell the user.
