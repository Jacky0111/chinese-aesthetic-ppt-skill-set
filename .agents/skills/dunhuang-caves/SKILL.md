# Dunhuang Caves

Use this skill when the user wants to design or revise a presentation inspired by Dunhuang cave art. Let content length, information density, logic, and narrative purpose determine each composition. Maintain coherence through mineral color layers, material texture, spatial depth, and flowing rhythm. Read [`assets/README.md`](assets/README.md) before designing; consult [`references/sources.md`](references/sources.md) as needed for dates, pigments, cave structure, or Silk Road exchange.

## Design language

- **Color:** Build a muted palette from sand yellow, ochre red, and stone green. Create depth through tonal variation, adjacent-color transitions, and localized pigment deposits. Adjust proportions to the content; do not present one cave or period's historical pigments as a universal palette. This palette is a modern design interpretation, not historical color reconstruction.
- **Mural and rock texture:** Suggest fine mineral granulation, matte pigment layers, natural color variation, subtle erosion, and rock grain. Keep the surface refined and restrained. Avoid distressed filters, dirty noise, torn-paper effects, or cheap cracks; do not reduce the style to a flat sand-yellow background.
- **Space and light:** Use foreground/background layering, partial occlusion, gradual depth, soft raking light, and whitespace to evoke a quiet, deep space. Do not draw cave entrances, repeated openings, cliffside cave façades, earthen forts, battlements, watchtowers, city walls, or other fortress-like architecture. Do not show a complete cave building.
- **Compositional rhythm:** Abstract the inward order, hierarchy, and geometric cadence of caisson ceilings into information grouping or local focal structure. Do not reproduce a specific caisson pattern or force circles and symmetry onto every slide. Translate the expansiveness and motion of apsara art into gentle lines, color flow, or page rhythm—never into apsara figures or literal ribbons.
- **Content adaptation:** Use timelines, narrative progression, or comparisons for historical development; material layering, process diagrams, or comparisons for pigment and conservation topics; and relationship paths, networks, or spatial storytelling for Silk Road exchange. These are options, not templates. Choose charts, tables, cards, and components from the actual content.
- **Imagery boundary:** Do not use Buddha figures, apsaras, lotus flowers, Buddhist symbols, complete murals, cave exteriors, or fortress-like forms as decoration. If the input must discuss one of these subjects, prefer text or abstract structure; consider literal depiction only if the user explicitly requests it, with source and scope checked.
- **Typography and readability:** A restrained Chinese Song-style display face may suit headings; use a clear modern typeface for body text and labels. Choose from fonts available in the environment; do not depend on personal font installations. Keep body text, footnotes, and chart labels on stable, low-texture fields away from dense grain, highlights, and color boundaries. Add a reading field, reduce local texture, or increase whitespace as needed.
- **Historical scope:** Specific caves, periods, pigments, and conservation states vary. Do not generalize features from one cave to all Dunhuang caves. Colors, textures, and spatial treatments in generated examples are modern interpretations, not artifact images, mural reconstructions, or restorations of historical appearance. See [`references/sources.md`](references/sources.md).
