# Dunhuang Caves: Historical Sources and Design Boundaries

## Traceable sources

- [UNESCO World Heritage Centre: Mogao Caves](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/440/) — Records that Mogao was first constructed in 366 AD, represents Buddhist art from the 4th to 14th centuries, and preserves 492 caves and about 45,000 square metres of murals. It also describes the site's position at a crossroads of Silk Road trade, religious, and cultural exchange.
- [Dunhuang Academy: “Research Progress on Pigments and Analysis Technology of Murals in Dunhuang Mogao Grottoes”](https://skytyz.dha.ac.cn/CN/Y2022/V1/I1/47) — Reviews pigments and analytical methods for Mogao murals. Specific pigments, techniques, and changes must be assessed against the cave, period, and analytical evidence.
- [Dunhuang Academy: Mogao Cave 407](https://www.dha.ac.cn/info/1425/3569.htm) — Documents the form and caisson ceiling of one specific Sui-period cave. This case informs an abstract understanding of hierarchy, inward order, and decorative rhythm; it must not be generalized to all Dunhuang caves.

## Evidence, interpretation, and unknowns

- **Verified facts:** UNESCO's construction date, art period, cave count, and mural area refer to Mogao. Dunhuang Academy pigment research addresses pigment types, analytical methods, and conservation. Cave 407's form and imagery are specific to that cave.
- **Modern design interpretation:** The combination and proportions of sand yellow, ochre red, and stone green; the emphasis on mineral grain; pigment transitions; subtle erosion; spatial light; caisson hierarchy; and apsara-inspired movement are modern design choices, not historical pigment recipes, measured colors, or artifact reconstruction.
- **Unknowns and limits:** The previews do not sample pigments, measure historical colors, or survey a particular cave. They cannot establish which mineral corresponds to a displayed color, the original hue of a specific mural, or precise cave geometry. Claims about pigments, dates, or imagery require cave-specific, traceable research.

## Use principles

Integrate material, space, and rhythm into information design rather than using religious imagery or complete murals as decoration. Treat a cave's pattern, pigment, or form as evidence only when the content requires it, and state the object and source. The style itself does not authorize reproducing artifact images or reference compositions.
