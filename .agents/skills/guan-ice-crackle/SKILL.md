# Guan Ware Ice Crackle

Design page structures around the supplied content, then use Guan ware’s visual language for consistency. Read `assets/README.md` first and consult relevant examples as needed. Use `references/sources.md` for historical claims and distinguish collection evidence from modern design interpretation.

## Visual language

- **Color:** Powder celadon, gray-green, pale moon-white, and warm gray form the base. Use restrained iron gray or muted brown-gray for edge and tonal accents. Treat powder celadon as a variable family, not a fixed swatch.
- **Material:** Suggest the softness, clarity, and depth of a thick glaze. Favor large irregular ice-crackle cells with occasional finer lines. Do not borrow Ding ware tear marks, Ru ware fine crackle, or Ge ware gold-and-iron crackle conventions.
- **Layout and hierarchy:** Choose composition from content density, logic, and emphasis. Texture may enter backgrounds, cards, charts, or dividers; lower contrast behind dense text. Examples are visual evidence, not templates.
- **Type and components:** Song-inspired display type can establish an elegant tone; use clear contemporary sans-serif or readable Song-style text for body copy. Design charts, tables, and timelines for their information rather than forcing a fixed component style.
- **Objects:** Convey the style through abstract glaze and crackle. Do not generate ceramic objects by default; include them only when the content calls for one.
