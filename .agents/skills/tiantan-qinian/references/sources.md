# Temple of Heaven Qinian: Historical Sources and Visual Interpretation

## Verified facts

- The initial principal building completed in 1420 was the rectangular Great Sacrificial Hall, used for joint offerings to Heaven and Earth. The present hall's form should not be projected back onto the 1420 structure.
- A spring prayer-for-harvest rite was established at the site from 1531. The circular Hall of Great Enjoyment was built there in 1545 and renamed the Hall of Prayer for Good Harvests in 1751.
- Ming and Qing emperors used the hall for spring prayers for a good harvest. The circular hall is associated with the idea of round Heaven, while its blue glazed tiles evoke the sky.
- The hall was destroyed by lightning in 1889 and rebuilt afterward.

## Modern visual interpretation

Sapphire, cinnabar, gilt, and Han-white/marble tones form a contemporary presentation palette; they are not measured reconstructions of historic pigments, materials, or lighting. Blue gradients and glaze-like sheen suggest celestial depth; cinnabar fields translate vertical order; fine gold lines and highlights guide attention; marble-like layers provide space and stability. Offset arcs, inward layers, and the balance of circle and square are modern abstractions, not architectural drawings.
