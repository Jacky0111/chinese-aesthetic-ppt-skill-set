# Palace Caisson

Let the content determine page structure and information density. Build recognition through layered space, mineral stone-blue and stone-green tones, restrained gold, and the patina of historic painted surfaces. Read [`assets/README.md`](assets/README.md) before designing. Historical evidence and interpretation boundaries are in [`references/sources.md`](references/sources.md).

## Visual language

- **Color:** Use stone blue and stone green as the main color layers, with gold reserved for limited focal accents, fine rules, or highlights. Adjust proportions to the content; do not present this palette as a universal historical scheme for all palace caissons. Warm gray, paper tones, or deep ink may support legibility.
- **Material:** Translate mineral pigment granularity, layered edges, subtle wear, and accumulated time into the visual system. Mural-like texture is a modern visual interpretation; it does not imply that caissons are made from mural materials. Let texture support the page rather than appear only as an isolated ornament.
- **Spatial hierarchy:** Derive depth, focus, and information hierarchy from the caisson's layered and upward-converging structure. Abstract square, octagonal, and circular relationships only when useful to the content; they are not required on every slide and should not become repeated frames or badges.
- **Figurative motifs:** The examples do not reproduce the dragon holding a pearl in the Hall of Supreme Harmony caisson. Unless a page specifically discusses a historical motif and a literal image is needed for understanding, avoid dragons, flowers, auspicious animals, or complete caisson patterns. Prefer their color relations, density, structural rhythm, and spatial mood.
- **Content adaptation:** Explain structure with an exploded view, hierarchy labels, or comparison; present history and conservation with timelines, material layers, or evidence cards; use a strong title, concise annotations, and open space for argumentative content. Layout, components, and density must change with the input; do not reuse example pages as templates.
- **Professional typography:** Maintain clear contrast for headings, body copy, annotations, and charts over textured surfaces. Keep small text away from gold highlights, dense pigment grain, and abrupt tonal boundaries. Add a stable reading field or reduce local texture when needed.
- **Historical boundaries:** Caissons vary by period, building, and craft tradition. Do not generalize a single example. Pigment evidence for stone blue and stone green comes from specific architectural painting samples and is not automatically a pigment analysis of the Hall of Supreme Harmony caisson. Consult [`references/sources.md`](references/sources.md) for claims.

## Using the resources

Read [`assets/README.md`](assets/README.md) first. When visual calibration is useful, consult the relevant image in `assets/examples/`. Extract color, material, texture, hierarchy, and mood only; never copy the full composition, wording, or element placement. Let the actual content drive each page.
