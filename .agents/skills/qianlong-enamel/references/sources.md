# Qianlong Enamel: Sources and Interpretation Boundaries

## Historical references

- [Palace Museum: Enamel-painted plum and peony bowl and process notes](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/ceramic/227499.html): terminology and process context for porcelain-body enamel painting.
- [Palace Museum: Qianlong-marked enamel floral bottle](https://intl.dpm.org.cn/Ceramicsis/81104.html): one collection example for pigments and decoration; not representative of every Qianlong-period enamel ware.
- [Palace Museum: Enamel landscape-and-poem bottle](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/ceramic/227071.html): collection and process context, not a slide motif template.
- [Palace Museum: Rouge-purple sgraffito enamel bottle](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/ceramic/227721.html): one example of ground color and decorative technique; do not copy its motifs.
- [The Metropolitan Museum of Art: mid-18th-century overglaze-enamel and gilt dish](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/50034): background on related overglaze enamels and gilding; its classification terminology is not interchangeable with “enamel-painted porcelain” in every context.

## Verified facts, design interpretation, and uncertainty

- **Verified:** Palace Museum materials use the term “瓷胎画珐琅” for enamel-painted porcelain and document its Qing court context. Individual objects vary in enameling, inscriptions, motifs, and gilding.
- **Modern interpretation:** This Skill uses the user-requested palette of blush pink, turquoise, and gold, with porcelain-white space and deep-ink text. Abstract color layers, glaze glints, precise detail, and contemporary typography convey luxury, refinement, and elegance. This is a design language, not a universal historical palette or reconstruction.
- **Caution:** Terms such as enamel-painted porcelain, famille rose, and yangcai vary across historical contexts and modern cataloguing. Verify object-specific claims against the relevant collection record; do not generalize from one object.

## Use principles

Extract color relationships, gloss, precision, layering, and artistic character. Do not transfer flowers, dragons, landscapes, figures, poems, seals, or borders from a reference object into slides merely because they appear on it. Use a literal motif only when the content discusses it or it is indispensable for comprehension, and state the source and scope.
