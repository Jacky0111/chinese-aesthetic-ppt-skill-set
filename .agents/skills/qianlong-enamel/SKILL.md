## Visual language

- **Color:** Use blush pink, turquoise, and porcelain white as the main palette, with deep ink for body text and gold as a small, precise accent. Adjust proportions for the content; do not claim this requested modern palette is a fixed formula for all Qianlong enamel wares.
- **Material and gloss:** Suggest refined enamel through translucent color layers, soft glaze sheen, fine grain, and natural tonal variation. Abstract washes, edges, and layered rhythms are sufficient; ceramic objects and crackle are not required.
- **Character:** Luxurious, intricate, elegant, and ordered. Create richness through color proportion, localized detail, whitespace, and hierarchy. Avoid crowded decoration, all-over gold, and faux-antique framing.
- **Layout:** Let content determine composition. Choose columns, processes, timelines, palette analyses, charts, or open title pages according to information relationships. Do not fix title placement, band direction, or card shapes. Vary layouts while preserving color, material, and refinement.
- **Readability:** Keep body copy, small labels, and annotations on stable low-texture fields with sufficient contrast. Keep text clear of highlights, complex color haze, and gold lines; reduce local texture or add a solid/translucent reading field when needed.
- **Motif boundary:** Do not transfer flowers, dragons, landscapes, figures, poems, seals, or ornamental borders from reference wares into slides merely because they appear there. Extract tone, line rhythm, density, material, and artistic character, then express them through abstract modern design. Use a literal motif only when the content discusses it or it is indispensable for comprehension; cite its source.

- **Scope:** This section governs presentation content and layout. Layout advice, including calls for short captions, does not authorize cutting or condensing the user's wording.
