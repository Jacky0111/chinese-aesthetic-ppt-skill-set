# Huizhou Architecture

Use this skill when the user wants to design or revise a presentation inspired by Huizhou architectural aesthetics. Let content length, information density, logic, and narrative purpose determine each slide. Maintain a recognizable style through tonal black-white-gray relationships, physical volume, natural materials, and spatial depth. Read [`assets/README.md`](assets/README.md) before designing; consult [`references/sources.md`](references/sources.md) as needed for historical, regional, or craft claims.

## Design language

- **Color:** Build the palette from ink black, white plaster, and dark roof-tile gray. Establish nuance through value and material response. Keep the whole slide in black, white, and gray, including sky and daylight, with no blue, green, or warm yellow hues, unless the user's content or an explicit request calls for another color. Adjust the balance to the content while preserving clear reading areas.
- **Volume and space:** Translate layered roof tiles, roof pitch and projection, stepped gable walls, wall transitions, and courtyard depth into thick planes, volumes, and spatial relationships. Create depth with foreground, middle ground, background, perspective, occlusion, support, and offset layers. Do not show a complete building or use roof outlines as stickers or repeated borders.
- **Framing boundary:** Use fragments only: close views of tiles, eaves, wall transitions, stone, and light form the page space, with broad wall planes, whitespace, or abstract volumes carrying the content. Do not render a recognizable whole building or a full courtyard scene (ground, several enclosing walls, and sky in one photographic view), and do not add moon gates, lattice doors or windows, plants, or blue sky. Show a specific object only when the user's content discusses it.
- **Material and light:** Suggest nuanced tile layering, matte plaster, natural gray stone, soft ambient light, contact shadows, edge highlights, and tonal transitions. Avoid flat fills, 2D silhouettes, flat zigzags, paper-like stacking, and isolated 3D ornament added for its own sake.
- **Composition and order:** Use staggered heights, diagonal extension, partial enclosure, framing, and whitespace to organize a page. Let spatial structure integrate with the title, reading area, charts, or components and their hierarchy. Do not require the same eave, wall, or 3D shape on every slide.
- **Content adaptation:** Historical sequences may use narrative progression or comparison; courtyard space may use a relationship diagram or layered explanation; craft, material, and regional differences may use editorial grouping, comparison, or concise labels. These are options only: choose components and layouts from the actual information.
- **Character:** Preserve elegance, quiet, restraint, and the refined feel of contemporary architectural editorial design. Let structured information coexist with abstract spatial atmosphere. Avoid forced sharp corners, fixed symmetry, or empty monochrome backgrounds.
- **Readability:** Place body text, footnotes, and chart labels on stable, low-texture areas. Keep text clear of tile detail, shadow boundaries, and highlights. Reduce local texture, add a reading field, or increase whitespace as needed; material effects must not compromise contrast.
- **Historical scope:** Huizhou architecture varies by region, period, and construction. Do not generalize specific cases such as Xidi, Hongcun, or Huizhou Three Carvings to all Huizhou architecture. See [`references/sources.md`](references/sources.md) for evidence, scope, and interpretation boundaries.

## Multilingual output

This section takes precedence over any other statement in this skill about language, typefaces, or reading direction.

- **Instruction language is not output language:** This file is written in English; it does not set the language of the presentation.
- **Choosing the output language automatically:** Follow the user's explicit choice. Otherwise infer it from the language and intent of the current input (slide content, request wording, context). No language is fixed, and neither the repository nor the instruction language is a default. Any language and mixed-language content are in scope.
- **Preserve the user's wording:** Never translate, rewrite, convert between Simplified and Traditional Chinese, or transliterate supplied content on your own initiative, including names, quotations, code, numbers, and units. Translate only when explicitly asked. Supplied text must appear character for character, including punctuation, capitalization, numbers, and units: add no terminal punctuation and swap no marks; line breaks may move but must not add or remove characters. If the requested output language differs from the supplied text and translation was not mentioned, no fixed follow-up question is needed: decide the language of newly written text from the user's input and keep the supplied text as it is.
- **Fit layout to the text:** Adjust type hierarchy, line breaks, columns, and slide count to actual text length. Do not force content in by shrinking it past legibility or clipping it.
- **Font coverage:** Use an available font that covers the target script, with no missing or invented glyphs. Where this skill names Song-style or sans-serif character, pick a serif or sans-serif of similar character for other scripts.
- **Reading direction:** For right-to-left scripts such as Arabic and Hebrew, mirror alignment, reading order, timelines, and process flow, keeping numbers, Latin words, and punctuation correct in mixed text. Use vertical setting only when the script supports it and the content calls for it.
- **Scope:** This section governs presentation content and layout. Layout advice, including calls for short captions, does not authorize cutting or condensing the user's wording. If supplied text conflicts with checkable history or this skill's historical boundaries, point it out and change it only after the user confirms.
- **Change the language, keep the style:** Preserve this skill's color, material, texture, gloss, depth, character, and atmosphere. Chinese text in example images is sample text: do not copy it or treat it as the default language.
- **Check before delivery:** When calling image generation, quote the slide text exactly in the prompt and state that no punctuation may be added, removed, or replaced. Before delivery, compare the text actually rendered with the user's text character by character, including the end of every line; fix or disclose any difference, and report what the image really shows. Also look for missing glyphs, misspellings, broken lines, and direction errors. If image generation cannot render a script reliably, use editable text layers; if that is not possible, tell the user.
