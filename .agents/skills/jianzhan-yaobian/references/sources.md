# Jian Ware Yaobian: Sources and Boundaries

## Verified collection information

- The Palace Museum relates Jian ware black-glazed tea bowls to Song-dynasty tea competitions and lists different visual types, including hare’s fur, partridge-feather, and Yaobian.
- The Palace Museum’s Yaobian records describe a black-glaze ground, spots, and blue halos. Descriptions of how viewing angle and light affect the halos should be tied to the specific object record.

## Historical claims to handle carefully

The historical use of the term Yaobian, explanations of its formation, and the scope and number of surviving examples may involve scholarly discussion. This Skill makes no single-cause or fixed-surviving-count claim, and does not generalize one object’s appearance to all Jian ware black-glazed vessels. Generated previews are not historical evidence.

## Modern visual interpretation

The near-black ground, shadowy blue and blue-violet halos, localized gradients, and crystal-spot details translate collection descriptions into a screen-based visual language; they are not precise glaze reconstructions. Silver-white highlights are a modern presentation-design choice for readability and hierarchy, not a claim about historical glaze color. Screen glow, typography color, and component texture are also modern interpretations.
