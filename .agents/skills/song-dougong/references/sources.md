# Song-Style Dougong: Sources and Design Interpretation

## Traceable sources

- [National Museum of China, Yingzao Fashi](https://www.chnmuseum.cn/zp/zpml/gjwxbt/202203/t20220301_254037.shtml) — Describes the work compiled by Li Jie as completed in 1100, with textual prescriptions and illustrated details including dougong and beams and columns.
- [The Palace Museum, Yingzao Fashi](https://www.dpm.org.cn/ancient/mingqing/149329.html) — Records that Li Jie revised the work by imperial order in 1097, completed the revision in 1100, and saw it printed in 1103; it also explains that major timber work used cai as a basis, divided into eight grades.
- [Palace Museum Journal, “Research on the Controlling Dimensions of Major Timber Work in Yingzao Fashi” (No. 6, 2015)](https://img.dpm.org.cn/Uploads/File/2018/06/04/u5b15212a9a148.pdf) — Qualifies the scope of the cai-fen system: it primarily specifies dimensions for major timber-work components and should not be treated as a universal module controlling the overall bay width, depth, column height, and other dimensions of an entire building.
- [Journal of Tsinghua University Art Museum (No. 3, 2021)](https://www.artmuseum.tsinghua.edu.cn/xsyj/gk/202111/W020211102411379529715.pdf) — Discusses dougong and systems of proportion in Chinese architecture, quoting Liang Sicheng’s account of dougong as a transition and connection between beams and columns, with both structural and decorative roles. Treat this as scholarly discussion, not a substitute for evidence about a specific building or period.

## Facts, interpretation, and unknowns

- **Verified:** The Yingzao Fashi was revised in 1100 and printed in 1103. It records construction prescriptions, component dimensions, and drawings. Major timber work recognizes eight grades of cai.
- **Verified scope limit:** Cai-fen primarily governs component dimensions; it is not a universal proportional system for every overall dimension of every building.
- **Design interpretation:** Mapping bearing, transition, connection, progression, and scale relationships to page hierarchy, component links, visual rhythm, and material layers is a modern design translation, not a historical prescription for slide composition.
- **Modern design choice:** Wood brown, blue-gray, and ivory define this skill’s visual palette. They do not claim a single universal color scheme for Song dougong or Song architecture. Texture, light, and spatial effects are also contemporary interpretations.
- **Unknowns and scope:** Song architecture varies by region, use, rank, period, and later repair. One text or building cannot represent every Song-style dougong system. Add specific sources when making claims about a particular construction detail or building; do not infer historic dimensions or appearance from generated examples.
