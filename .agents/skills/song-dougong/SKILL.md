# Song-Style Dougong Presentation Design

Use this skill when designing or revising a presentation inspired by Song-style dougong. Let the page respond to the input’s length, information density, relationships, and narrative purpose. Before designing, read [assets/README.md](assets/README.md). Consult [references/sources.md](references/sources.md) when making historical or architectural claims.

## Visual language

- **Color:** Use warm wood brown for visual grounding, blue-gray for cool structure, and ivory for readable space. Adjust their balance to the content. This is a modern design palette, not a historical color survey of Song architecture. Avoid full-page brown, empty solid-color backgrounds, or texture that competes with text.
- **Bearing and connection:** Translate dougong’s bearing, transition, and linking relationships into information hierarchy, component connections, visual emphasis, and points of support. Make the page’s parts relate to one another; do not turn dou, gong, and ang into separate decorative objects.
- **Interlock and progression:** Use offset, overlap, occlusion, foreground/background layers, selective extension, and guided movement to suggest organized relationships. Choose curved surfaces, horizontal layers, or local rhythms according to the content. Do not repeat one geometric shape across every slide or substitute an exploded component diagram for a complete page.
- **Module and proportion:** “Eight grades of cai” may inspire related scales in type, spacing, alignment, and component sizing, but only as a design analogy. In the Yingzao Fashi, the cai-fen system primarily specifies dimensions for major timber-work components; do not describe it as a universal proportional system governing the whole building. Do not mechanically turn the eight grades into eight modules on every page.
- **Material and depth:** Suggest thickness with restrained fine wood grain, matte blue-gray surfaces, soft raking light, contact shadows, and occlusion. Texture may enter backgrounds, reading fields, or components only when it supports information. Avoid distressed-paper styling, harsh glare, flat color alone, and stacks of literal building parts.
- **Song atmosphere:** Aim for clarity, rationality, restraint, precision, and varied density. Achieve this through proportion, spatial relationships, rhythm, and detail rather than calligraphy, faux-antique borders, or generic traditional motifs.
- **Content adaptation:** Chronology may use a timeline; standards and measures may use hierarchy or editorial comparison; structural meaning may use a relationship diagram or staggered narrative; dense content needs clearly organized reading areas. These are options, not fixed layouts. Design charts, tables, processes, and information components for the actual content.
- **Legibility:** Place body copy, small labels, and chart notes in areas with stable contrast and restrained texture. Keep them clear of high-contrast grain, strong light, and material boundaries. Add an ivory reading field, reduce local texture, or adjust contrast when needed.

## Using the resources

Read assets/README.md before designing. Consult only the relevant examples in assets/examples/ when calibrating material, structural layering, or page roles. Extract color relationships, material depth, bearing rhythm, negative space, and information hierarchy; do not reproduce a full example composition, its wording, or element placement. ## Historical scope

“Song-style dougong” does not describe one form universal to all regions, building types, and periods. Follow the source and scope notes in [references/sources.md](references/sources.md) when citing the Yingzao Fashi, construction standards, or a specific building. Distinguish documented history from modern design interpretation.

## Multilingual output

This section takes precedence over any other statement in this skill about language, typefaces, or reading direction.

- **Instruction language is not output language:** This file is written in English; it does not set the language of the presentation.
- **Choosing the output language automatically:** Follow the user's explicit choice. Otherwise infer it from the language and intent of the current input (slide content, request wording, context). No language is fixed, and neither the repository nor the instruction language is a default. Any language and mixed-language content are in scope.
- **Preserve the user's wording:** Never translate, rewrite, convert between Simplified and Traditional Chinese, or transliterate supplied content on your own initiative, including names, quotations, code, numbers, and units. Translate only when explicitly asked. Supplied text must appear character for character, including punctuation, capitalization, numbers, and units: add no terminal punctuation and swap no marks; line breaks may move but must not add or remove characters. If the requested output language differs from the supplied text and translation was not mentioned, no fixed follow-up question is needed: decide the language of newly written text from the user's input and keep the supplied text as it is.
- **Fit layout to the text:** Adjust type hierarchy, line breaks, columns, and slide count to actual text length. Do not force content in by shrinking it past legibility or clipping it.
- **Font coverage:** Use an available font that covers the target script, with no missing or invented glyphs. Where this skill names Song-style or sans-serif character, pick a serif or sans-serif of similar character for other scripts.
- **Reading direction:** For right-to-left scripts such as Arabic and Hebrew, mirror alignment, reading order, timelines, and process flow, keeping numbers, Latin words, and punctuation correct in mixed text. Use vertical setting only when the script supports it and the content calls for it.
- **Scope:** This section governs presentation content and layout. Layout advice, including calls for short captions, does not authorize cutting or condensing the user's wording. If supplied text conflicts with checkable history or this skill's historical boundaries, point it out and change it only after the user confirms.
- **Change the language, keep the style:** Preserve this skill's color, material, texture, gloss, depth, character, and atmosphere. Chinese text in example images is sample text: do not copy it or treat it as the default language.
- **Check before delivery:** When calling image generation, quote the slide text exactly in the prompt and state that no punctuation may be added, removed, or replaced. Before delivery, compare the text actually rendered with the user's text character by character, including the end of every line; fix or disclose any difference, and report what the image really shows. Also look for missing glyphs, misspellings, broken lines, and direction errors. If image generation cannot render a script reliably, use editable text layers; if that is not possible, tell the user.
