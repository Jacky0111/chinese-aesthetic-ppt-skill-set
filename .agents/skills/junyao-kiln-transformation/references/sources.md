# Jun Ware Kiln Transformation: Sources and Boundaries

## Verified collection evidence

- Palace Museum records describe Jun ware using iron and copper colorants to produce blue with red, purple patches, pure sky blue, and pale moon-white. The blue glaze has an opalescent, opaque appearance.
- Palace Museum Jun ware objects show sky-blue glaze with purple-red patches. Some Jun glaze surfaces have irregular flowing fine lines known as “earthworm tracks” (`蚯蚓走泥纹`).
- “Jun ware” covers different periods and kiln sites. The color, shape, or technique of one object should not be treated as a universal property of all Jun ware.

## Design interpretation

Flowing screen gradients, soft light, purple-red accents, and deep-violet focal areas are contemporary interpretations. Do not present them as exact reconstructions of ancient glaze or reduce kiln transformation to one fixed recipe or flame motif.
