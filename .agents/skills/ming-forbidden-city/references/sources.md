# Ming Forbidden City: Sources and Interpretation Boundaries

## Historical sources

- [The Palace Museum: Yongle Emperor](https://www.dpm.org.cn/court/lineage/226257.html): Records the decision to build palace buildings in Beijing in 1406, large-scale construction in 1417, completion in 1420, and the northern-frontier context.
- [The Palace Museum: Reconstruction of the Three Great Halls](https://www.dpm.org.cn/lemmas/243698.html): Records the 1421 fire and reconstruction beginning in 1440 and finishing in 1441.
- [The Palace Museum: A Brief Account of Palace Repairs in the Forbidden City](https://www.dpm.org.cn/building/talk/224298.html): Supports the general construction chronology and building-history context.
- [The Palace Museum: Roof Tile Inspection Begins at the Hall of Supreme Harmony](https://www.dpm.org.cn/classify_detail/160353.html): Provides material on the Hall of Supreme Harmony roof, glazed tiles, and components. Verify form and age against the specific building and component.

## Evidence, modern interpretation, and unknowns

- **Verified facts:** Ming-period dates and events used in these examples come from the Palace Museum sources above. Existing buildings underwent later rebuilding, repair, and alteration; they should not all be described as original Ming structures.
- **Modern interpretation:** The proportions of cinnabar, imperial gold, and glazed white, along with mineral texture, angled warm light, and long shadows, are a modern visual language—not a colorimetric reconstruction of historical pigments or surfaces.
- **Unknowns and limits:** This Skill does not reconstruct the color of each building or component in the Ming period. Verify the building, component, material date, and repair record before making specific claims; do not infer original Ming appearance from current conditions alone.

## Use principle

Extract color relationships, material, light, spatial layers, and rhythm from the architectural setting. Do not add eaves, dougong brackets, dragons, or other ornaments merely because they appear on historical buildings. Use literal forms only when the content discusses them and their depiction aids understanding; cite the source and date range.
